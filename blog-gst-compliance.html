B2B GST Compliance & Tax Advisory in India: A Startup Playbook

By Neha Gupta, CA • July 25, 2026 • 6 Min Read

Navigating the complex landscape of Goods and Services Tax (GST) is essential for any expanding startup in India. Non-compliance often triggers penalties and causes billing friction with B2B corporate clients who expect clean Input Tax Credit (ITC) reconciliation. This guide highlights the core compliance pillars for founders.

1. When is GST Registration Mandatory?

Startups providing digital or physical services must register for GST once their aggregate turnover exceeds ₹20 Lakhs in a financial year (₹10 Lakhs for special category states). If you are trading physical items across state boundaries, registration is required from inception, regardless of turnover thresholds.

2. Securing Input Tax Credit (ITC)

Input Tax Credit allows your business to deduct the GST paid on purchases (like cloud servers, SaaS software licenses, and engineering office rentals) from the GST collected on your customer invoices. To claim ITC safely, ensure that your vendors submit their GST returns timely, letting transaction details display in your GSTR-2B reconciliation log.

"Failing to reconcile your GSTR-2B log monthly is one of the most common causes of cash leaks in growth-stage companies. Set up structured checks."

3. Essential Filing Schedules

Under GST rules, startups generally file monthly returns, including GSTR-1 (sales details due by the 11th of the following month) and GSTR-3B (tax liability summary and payment due by the 20th). Smaller companies can choose the QRMP (Quarterly Return Monthly Payment) system to reduce return frequency.

4. E-Invoicing & Invoice Matching

If your annual company turnover exceeds ₹5 Crores, generating e-invoices directly through the government portal is mandatory for all B2B transactions. Non-e-invoiced bills are legally invalid, preventing your corporate clients from claiming their ITC. Automated invoicing tools ensure billing runs without errors.
